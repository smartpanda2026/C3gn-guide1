What is C3 Glomerulopathy?

C3G is a histologic pattern of glomerulonephritis defined by C3-dominant glomerular staining on immunofluorescence — C3 at least two orders of magnitude greater than any other immune reactant — with minimal or no immunoglobulin. [2][3]

Electron microscopy subclassifies C3G into dense deposit disease (DDD) and C3 glomerulonephritis (C3GN). Both are driven by dysregulation of the alternative pathway (AP) of complement. [2][7][8]

Epidemiology

  • Annual incidence 0.2–2 / million
  • Prevalence 0.05–1.4 / 10,000
  • Hypocomplementemia ~50% only
  • Progression to ESKD 30–50% / 10 yr

Hypocomplementemia is present in only ~50% — a normal C3 does not exclude C3G.[1][7]

Pathogenesis

Alternative Pathway Dysregulation

C3G arises from uncontrolled AP activation. Drivers include:

Genetic variants (CFH, CD46, C3, CFB)
C3 nephritic factor (C3NeF)
AP autoantibodies
Infection triggers
Monoclonal gammopathy / paraprotein (esp. >50 yr)
Clinical Presentation

Signs & Symptoms

  • Hematuria (macroscopic or microscopic)
  • Proteinuria / nephrotic syndrome
  • Edema
  • Hypertension
  • Declining kidney function

Diagnostic Workup — Step by Step

Click each step to expand the details. Biopsy is required; C3G is a histologic diagnosis.

Immunofluorescence (IF) — the defining test

Bright C3 staining with minimal or no immunoglobulin. C3 must be at least 2 orders of magnitude greater than any other immune reactant.[1][2][3]

Electron microscopy (EM) — subclassification

DDD: highly osmophilic intramembranous dense deposits. C3GN: mesangial, subendothelial, subepithelial and/or intramembranous deposits.[2][7][3]

Light microscopy (LM) — variable pattern

Mesangial proliferative, endocapillary proliferative, membranoproliferative (MPGN), or necrotizing/crescentic.[2][7][3][8]

The 12-week rule — infection-related GN

If all features of GN resolve by ~12 weeks, the patient is not considered to have C3G (consensus).[1][9]

Monoclonal gammopathy / paraprotein

Evaluate particularly in patients >50 years, though paraproteins can occur in younger patients. Clone-directed therapy may improve outcomes.[1][9]

Masked monoclonal deposits

Consider pronase-digested paraffin immunofluorescence to unmask "masked" monoclonal deposits when small amounts of immunoglobulin accompany C3.[1][9]

KDIGO 2021 — Supportive & Immunosuppressive Treatment

No randomized trials informed the original KDIGO recommendations — they are based on retrospective cohorts and expert opinion.[1][2]

Select a disease scenario:

Supportive Care

Mild disease / all patients

Patients with preserved function, proteinuria <1.5 g/day, and no significant hematuria can be managed conservatively.[2][9]

  • RAAS blockade (ACEi/ARB) for BP and proteinuria control
  • Lipid management
  • Lifestyle measures (salt restriction, smoking cessation, weight)
KDIGO Practice Point 8.2.2.1

Moderate-to-Severe disease

Definition of moderate-to-severe:

Moderate-to-marked proliferation on biopsy with proteinuria >2 g/day.
Also consider treatment if proteinuria >1 g/day plus hematuria or declining function over ≥6 months.[1][2]

Initial: MMF + glucocorticoids

If treatment fails: consider eculizumab

KDIGO Practice Point 8.2.2.2

Refractory disease

Enroll in a clinical trial where available.[1]

Given the expanding landscape of targeted complement inhibitors, trial enrollment is increasingly relevant for patients failing conventional immunosuppression.

Secondary cause

Monoclonal Gammopathy–Associated C3G

When a paraprotein is identified, clone-directed therapy may improve kidney outcomes rather than conventional immunosuppression alone.[1][9]

Diagnostic workup for monoclonal gammopathy

  • Screen all adults, especially >50 years
  • Consider pronase-digested paraffin IF for masked deposits
  • Hematology referral for clone characterization (SPEP, SFLC, bone marrow biopsy)
  • Complement genetics to identify pathogenic variants (e.g., CFH, C3, CFB)

Clone-Directed Therapy — CyBorD Regimen (non-IgM monoclonal)

For non-IgM monoclonal immunoglobulin (e.g., IgG or IgA). The CyBorD regimen is the preferred approach for monoclonal gammopathy-associated C3G.

Drug Dose Schedule
Cyclophosphamide 300 mg/m² (max 500 mg) Days 1, 8, 15, 22 (oral or IV)
Bortezomib (Velcade®) 1.3 mg/m² Days 1, 8, 15, 22 (subcutaneous)
Dexamethasone 40 mg Days 1, 8, 15, 22 (oral or IV)

Cycle length: 28 days. Also known as VCd (Velcade, cyclophosphamide, dexamethasone). For dexamethasone, a reduced dose of 20 mg weekly may be used for patients >70 years, BMI <18.5, or those with poor tolerance. Bortezomib is preferentially administered subcutaneously to reduce peripheral neuropathy risk. Once-weekly bortezomib (1.3 mg/m² on days 1, 8, 15, 22) shows comparable efficacy with lower neuropathy rates compared to twice-weekly administration.

Clone-Directed Therapy — Rituximab-Based Regimen (IgM monoclonal)

For IgM monoclonal immunoglobulin (e.g., associated with Waldenström macroglobulinemia or B-cell lymphoma).

Drug Dose Schedule
Rituximab 375 mg/m² Weekly × 4 weeks (or 1,000 mg × 2 doses 2 weeks apart)
Cyclophosphamide (optional) 300 mg/m² Days 1, 8, 15, 22 (as part of combination)
Dexamethasone (optional) 40 mg Days 1, 8, 15, 22 (as part of combination)

Rituximab alone or in combination with cyclophosphamide and dexamethasone is recommended for IgM monoclonal gammopathy-associated C3G. Rituximab is preferred in pre-menopausal women concerned about fertility and in frail older adults.

Targeted Complement Inhibition

Two agents acting proximally on the alternative pathway now carry C3G indications — a major shift from terminal C5 blockade.

Iptacopan (Fabhalta®)

Factor B inhibitor · Oral

FDA approved

APPEAR-C3G · Phase 3

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of oral iptacopan in C3G.[5]

Dosing Schedule

Adults 200 mg PO twice daily
Total daily dose 400 mg
Administration With or without food; swallow whole
Renal impairment No dose adjustment (eGFR ≥25)

Used in combination with a RAS inhibitor (unless contraindicated/intolerable).

  • Significant proteinuria reduction at 6 months, sustained to 12 months
  • First disease-specific therapy for C3G (2025)

Pre-Transplant Planning

Complement genetics

Identify pathogenic variants (e.g., CFH, C3, CFB) that may inform donor selection and recurrence risk.[1][7][9]

Monoclonal gammopathy screen

Detect and treat paraprotein/clone before transplant where present.[1][9]

Donor selection

Avoid living-related donors carrying the same complement variant where feasible.[12]

Counsel on recurrence risk

Discuss high recurrence rate and potential need for complement inhibition post-transplant.[12]

Post-Transplant Management Algorithm

1

Surveillance

Monitor proteinuria, eGFR, hematuria, and C3 levels. Consider protocol biopsies, as histologic recurrence can precede clinical manifestations.[12]

2

Confirm recurrence on biopsy

Re-biopsy with IF and EM to confirm C3-dominant recurrence and exclude other causes of graft dysfunction (rejection, infection, drug toxicity).[12]

3

Optimize supportive care & immunosuppression

RAAS blockade, BP control, and review of baseline immunosuppression. Consider MMF-based regimens as per KDIGO principles.[1][2]

4

Targeted complement inhibition

Pegcetacoplan is indicated for post-transplant recurrent C3G (C3/C3b inhibitor; 1,080 mg SC twice weekly; 68.1% relative proteinuria reduction in VALIANT, benefit consistent across transplant status).[6]

Iptacopan (200 mg PO twice daily) also showed favorable results in 11 kidney transplant patients in a phase 2 trial. Eculizumab has been used historically but with inconsistent benefit.
5

Graft loss & re-transplantation

If graft failure occurs, re-transplantation may be considered. Recurrence risk persists, so pre-transplant complement workup and counseling remain essential.[12]

Key Evidence

  • C3G recurs frequently after kidney transplant (histologic recurrence up to ~89%) with high graft-loss risk, and the pegcetacoplan indication includes post-transplant recurrent disease.[12][6]
  • VALIANT benefit was consistent across disease type, transplant status, baseline kidney function, and concurrent immunosuppression.[6]
  • Praga et al. review native and post-transplant C3G, emphasizing the high recurrence burden and the need for optimized management strategies.[12]

Illustrative trajectories based on published C3G cohort data and iptacopan 39-month extension study

eGFR Proteinuria (g/day) ACR (mg/g)

eGFR (left axis)

Improving trajectory: +6 mL/min/1.73m² over 24 months. Pre-treatment slope typically −13.4 mL/min/1.73m²/year; treatment improves to −2.1 mL/min/1.73m²/year.

Proteinuria — g/day

Sustained reduction: 3.8 → 1.0 g/day (74% reduction). Iptacopan extension data showed 40% UPCR reduction at 39 months; VALIANT showed 68.1% reduction at week 26.

ACR (mg/g)

Parallel ACR reduction: 480 → 120 mg/g (75% reduction). ACR provides a more sensitive measure at low proteinuria levels, useful for detecting early response or relapse.

Monitoring intervals

Assess eGFR, proteinuria, and ACR at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 months. More frequent monitoring (monthly) during initiation or after dose changes.
